# Warranty, quality and after-sales support strategy

**Status:** Proposal for owner/legal review. No Opica warranty, maintenance rate, response target, unlimited support or guarantee is approved for publication.

## Customer expectations and competitor evidence

Common buyer concerns include: whether agreed functionality will work; what happens when a reproducible defect appears; who owns source code and accounts; whether documentation/training is included; how hosting/API failures are handled; whether security patches/backups are covered; and how to obtain support after launch.

Public examples reviewed:

- BizzForge advertises a 15-day website launch warranty restricted to technical faults attributable to its delivered build, excluding new features, third-party outages and client changes. [Terms](https://bizzforge.co.za/terms/)
- Empress Solutions publishes support periods of 14 days for custom websites, 30 for a portfolio buyout, 60 for ecommerce and 90 for custom/enterprise software, limited to bugs related to delivered work. [Terms](https://www.empresssolutions.co.za/terms)
- Softezm states a standard 30-calendar-day bug-fix warranty, with maintenance/support separately scoped after the window. [Terms](https://softezm.com/terms)
- Sitect states 60–90 days depending on the statement of work and excludes third-party/provider failures, new features and client modifications. [Terms](https://sitect.co.za/public/terms)
- Tchaditech advertises maintenance contracts, 24/7 monitoring, backups and support, but does not publish a warranty period on the reviewed page. [Site](https://tchaditech.com/)
- AfriCatalyst's development RFP defines priority-specific warranty response targets and milestone billing; it is a buyer procurement specification, not a competitor standard. [RFP](https://africatalyst.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/TOR-WEBSITE-DEVELOPMENT-SERVICES.pdf)

These policies are examples only; local law, contract language and project type may alter what can be promised.

## Proposed service boundaries

**A. Bug-fix warranty** — consider 30 calendar days beginning at accepted production launch or formal handover, whichever is explicitly selected in the contract. Covers reproducible failures in delivered functionality that do not conform to signed requirements under the agreed supported environment. Proposal only; owner and local legal counsel should approve the final definition.

**B. Maintenance** — separate paid recurring service with explicit included hours/tasks (e.g. agreed dependency/security updates, backups, uptime checks, small fixes), service window, response targets, monthly fee, overage, exclusions, access/security process, and cancellation terms. Do not describe maintenance as included unless included in the quote.

**C. Technical support** — define support channel, days/hours, severity, acknowledgement target, diagnosis/fix target or “best effort,” escalation and included quota. Support response is not the same as a guaranteed fix or project delivery date.

**D. New features and changed requirements** — outside warranty; estimate as new scope and approve in writing before work.

**E. Third-party failures** — hosting, DNS, app stores, payment processors, external APIs, client changes and provider policy changes are outside a developer's direct control. Offer diagnosis/coordination as a separately scoped service where feasible.

## Quality and handover items to define in each contract

- Test scope and acceptance criteria, supported browsers/devices and deployment environment.
- Defect reporting channel, required reproduction details, severity, warranty start/end and correction procedure.
- Source-code ownership/transfer after agreed payment, third-party/open-source licences, credentials and account ownership.
- Documentation, setup/deployment notes, data export/backup, admin training and handover checklist.
- Backups, security update duties, monitoring, incident notification and restore responsibility if a maintenance contract exists.
- External service fees, uptime dependencies and client responsibilities.
- Explicit exclusion for new features, changed requirements, unauthorized modification and unsupported environments.

## Proposed support tiers for costing, not publication

1. **Warranty window:** in-scope defects only, no routine content changes or new features.
2. **Basic care:** monthly dependency/security review and a defined small maintenance allowance, during named business hours.
3. **Managed support:** named severity levels, monitoring/backups and approved response windows, only if operational coverage exists.
4. **On-demand engineering:** separately billed diagnostics, integrations or feature work; quote before execution.

No tier should say “unlimited,” “lifetime,” “100% error-free,” or guarantee third-party uptime. Price tiers only after cost/capacity review.
