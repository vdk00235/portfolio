## Competitive evidence to use carefully

- **Chad published reference:** Adjemson's 2026 card lists 100,000/200,000/350,000 XAF one-off tiers for one/5/10-page websites, corresponding 12-month monthly payment options, and separately priced content, booking, CRM/ERP, chatbot and maintenance add-ons.
- **Chad quote-led reference:** Chad Connect and Tchaditech describe scope-based quotations rather than fixed public rates. Albiher also presents a quote-first site and timeline claims.
- **Regional FCFA references:** ELK Consulting publishes website from 50,000 XAF, e-commerce from 225,000, mobile app from 200,000 and custom platform from 275,000. It is Mauritius-based, so this is not a same-country peer.
- **Regional African and international context:** Snrael, EmmCloud and South African agencies publish package prices in USD/KES/ZAR; Upwork publishes hourly ranges in USD. Currency, taxes, scope, provider market and support differ substantially.

Use source-matched scopes. Do not convert these into one combined “Africa average,” and do not treat a competitor's entry package as the price for a custom product.

## Recommended commercial model to evaluate

1. **Fixed price for bounded, repeatable scope:** landing page, defined small website, or one clear integration, with page/feature limits, client inputs, rounds, hosting and support stated.
2. **Milestone fixed quote after discovery:** ecommerce, dashboards, mobile apps and workflow systems after screens, roles, data, integrations and acceptance criteria are written down.
3. **Time-and-materials or capped discovery for uncertain maintenance/legacy work:** set a rate, cap/approval steps, invoice cadence and access expectations.
4. **Monthly maintenance only as an opt-in defined service:** specify exact service hours/tasks, response schedule, overage and exclusions.
5. **Regional pricing:** consider separate local and international offer structures only if costs/payment methods/support differ; never use arbitrary nationality-based price discrimination. Quote currency, taxes, payment fees and exchange-rate validity openly.

## Price-setting worksheet

For each candidate service, the owner should record:

- Scope: included pages/screens/roles, platforms, languages, integrations, data and admin functions.
- Delivery effort: discovery, design, implementation, QA, deployment, handover and project coordination.
- Capacity and utilization: available billable hours, concurrency and time reserved for support.
- Costs: payment/bank fees, hosting, domain, store accounts, paid APIs, SMS/WhatsApp, licenses, tax and subcontractors.
- Risk: unclear requirements, third-party dependencies, client response assumptions and maintenance load.
- Profit floor: owner-approved gross margin/contribution after costs.
- Terms: deposit/milestones, quote validity, change requests, cancellation, acceptance, source ownership and warranty.

`price floor = delivery cost + direct costs + risk reserve + target contribution`

The formula is a planning tool, not an industry benchmark. Do not infer local cost-of-living or labor cost from web search rates.

## How to compete on value instead of discount

- Give a buyer a clear scope list and exclusions before work begins.
- Separate third-party charges and recurring costs from one-time build fees.
- Offer an economical option by reducing scope (e.g. fewer pages/features or one platform) rather than silently reducing QA or margin.
- Show a milestone demo/acceptance process only if it can actually be maintained.
- Explain client-side requirements and what changes add cost/time.
- Include handover and support only where their costs are in the quote.
- Show comparable packages only after the owner confirms fixed scope and healthy margins.

## Package clarity issues to resolve before price publication

For each proposed package specify page/screen/user limits; languages; content writing; responsive support; CMS/admin; ecommerce/catalog/payments; test scope; domain/hosting; third-party accounts/fees; SEO setup; revisions; client approvals; source code/IP; documentation/training; warranty window; maintenance; quote validity; taxes and payment terms. Identify any optional add-on and recurring fee.
