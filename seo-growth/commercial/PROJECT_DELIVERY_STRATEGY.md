# Project delivery strategy

**Status:** Proposed estimating framework; no Opica delivery commitments are approved.

## Competitor delivery evidence

- Adjemson & Consulting advertises 3 workdays for a one-page site, 15 for up to five pages and 25 for up to ten pages. The tiers assume differing scopes and inputs. [Rate card](https://tchad.adjemson.com/grille-tarifaire-creation-de-site-web/)
- Albiher Digital advertises one week for a simple showcase website and 1–3 weeks depending on complexity. [Homepage](https://albiherdigital.com/index.html)
- ELK Consulting lists 10–20 working days for its ecommerce tier; other mobile/custom lead times are confirmed after review. [Package page](https://www.elkconsultingltd.com/en/creation-site-web)
- We Write Code advertises 3–5 days for a landing page, 1–2 weeks for a five-page website, 2–4 weeks for a small ecommerce scope, and 6–8 weeks for a React Native MVP. [Pricing](https://wewritecode.co.za/pricing)
- Snrael states a starter MVP from three weeks. [Pricing](https://snrael.com/pricing)
- ITD GrowthLabs publishes longer indicative timelines: landing page 1–2 weeks, business site 4–8 weeks, mobile MVP 6–10 weeks, SaaS MVP 3–4 months; region and scope differ materially. [Africa market page](https://itdgrowthlabs.com/africa/index.php)

These are supplier marketing estimates, not independently observed project durations. “Workdays” differs from calendar weeks. Their client input, revision, QA and store-review assumptions are not standardized.

## Proposed estimating categories (internal planning bands only)

The bands below are starting **planning hypotheses**, not observed Opica delivery performance or public promises. They are deliberately broad and must be calibrated with actual project time logs and capacity.

| Category | Planning hypothesis after ready-to-start scope | Exclusions / important dependencies |
|---|---|---|
| Small: static landing page or bounded single workflow | 1–3 weeks | Client copy/assets, approvals, domain/hosting access and one consolidated review cycle on time |
| Medium: multi-page site, dashboard or modest ecommerce | 3–8 weeks | Product/data setup, payment provider availability, integrations, content migration, acceptance feedback |
| Complex: multi-role application, several integrations or custom business workflow | 8–20+ weeks, milestone plan | Discovery, security/privacy review, legacy data, external APIs and operating constraints; re-estimate after discovery |
| MVP: limited product slice with one primary platform and validated requirements | 6–12 weeks as a planning range | “MVP” needs a signed feature boundary; app-store review and third-party accounts are outside developer control |
| Long-term: SaaS, multi-phase custom software or evolving roadmap | Quarterly or milestone roadmap, not one completion date | Separate releases, ongoing priorities, support and scope change; no finite date before discovery |

No project dates, durations or time logs in the repo support these as Opica historical estimates. Keep these bands internal until the owner and delivery team approve them.

## Estimation protocol

1. Identify user/business outcome, in-scope workflows, roles, platforms, integrations and exclusions.
2. Separate discovery/design, implementation, test/fix, client review and deployment/handover effort.
3. Record assumptions for client content/data, decision-maker availability, payment/API access, hosting, accounts and legal requirements.
4. Quote a **range** while requirements are uncertain. Convert it to a contractual target only in a signed statement of work with milestones, dependencies, acceptance criteria, change control and client review windows.
5. Add contingency based on integration and requirement risk rather than using the smallest competitor's published time.
6. Review actual elapsed time versus estimated effort at project close; separate developer delays from client/external waiting time.

## Estimate vs guarantee

- **Estimated delivery:** planning range subject to assumptions and client responses.
- **Contractual target:** date/milestone defined in the signed agreement; remedy and dependencies are contract-specific.
- **Client-dependent delay:** late content, access, decisions, feedback or approvals can move dates under agreed pause rules.
- **Additional scope:** written change request with new cost and schedule before work begins.
- **Revision period:** use explicitly defined rounds and review windows, not unlimited changes.
- **Testing and deployment:** budget for functional QA and deployment; app store decisions, payment provider onboarding and external outages remain outside direct control.

## Delivery metrics to start collecting

For each project retain scope/version, estimated hours and calendar range, start/acceptance/launch dates, client waiting days, changes, defects at launch, support requests, and reason for variance. After 5–10 comparable deliveries, revise the planning bands. Do not use “fastest” claims; no comparative evidence could substantiate them.
