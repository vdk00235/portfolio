# Pricing competitiveness research

**Research date:** 23 November 2026  
**Scope:** Publicly available developer and agency pricing found for Chad, African markets and international freelance platforms. This is a directional sample, not a census. Prices are reported as published; currencies are not converted because exchange rates, taxes, service scope, location and date-specific costs can make a converted comparison misleading. The linked source is the authority for its own terms.

## Executive finding

No actual Opica Soft prices, billable hours, project cost base, payment terms, maintenance fees or margins were found in the repository. Therefore Opica's market position cannot yet be classified as cheap, affordable, competitive or expensive. There is no basis for recommending a price reduction.

The Chadian public market sample spans **quote-only** competitors and one published 2026 rate card with 100,000 FCFA one-page, 200,000 FCFA five-page and 350,000 FCFA ten-page website tiers, plus separately priced add-ons. A regional Mauritius-based provider publishes website starts at 50,000 FCFA, e-commerce at 225,000 FCFA, a mobile app at 200,000 FCFA, and custom platform at 275,000 FCFA. This spread shows that scope, hosting, content, payment integration, platform and support must be compared alongside the headline price.

International and African agency examples show a different scale and currency context. Upwork's 2026 guidance lists web developers at $15–50/hour and mobile app developers at $18–39/hour. Regional provider Snrael lists a landing page at $300, company website at $800, online store at $1,200 and app MVP at $1,500. These are contextual benchmarks, not appropriate direct price targets for Opica.

### Pricing evidence that remains unknown

For Chad-based providers other than the explicit Adjemson rate card, no verified website/app/API/mobile/custom-software prices were found in reviewed pages. Chad Connect, Tchaditech and Albiher largely position through scoping/quotes. For Opica, every price and term is unknown. Competitor taxes, exact payment terms, revision limits, source ownership, ongoing third-party charges and warranty detail are not consistently stated.

## Market interpretation

1. **Local commercial norm:** quote after scoping is a visible local pattern. A useful no-price site can still be transparent by explaining the factors that drive scope, offering a no-obligation scoping call only if the owner approves it, and returning a written itemized proposal.
2. **Published local anchor:** Adjemson's rate card is the strongest same-country evidence found. It creates a visible reference for small presentation sites, but differs in page count, content, hosting, language, workday assumptions and add-ons. It is not a valid apples-to-apples quote until those details align.
3. **FCFA low-price anchor:** ELK's published website/app starting prices are another explicit FCFA reference, but ELK is Mauritius-based and its final prices depend on quote/country. Treat it as a regional, not Chadian, competitor.
4. **Value differentiation:** fixed scope, line-item inclusions/exclusions, client-owned accounts, working demo, testing, handover, understandable documentation and a defined defect window can improve confidence without cutting the headline fee. Whether Opica can deliver these must first be confirmed.
5. **Strongest opportunity candidates:** small business web presence; tightly scoped ecommerce; single-codebase mobile MVP; and API/integration work appear to have visible buyer demand/pricing tiers. Evidence is insufficient to say they are Opica's strongest-margin services. AI, SaaS, automation, business systems and complex backend are not justified as specialties by current evidence.
6. **Do not price by cheapest competitor:** a 100k FCFA one-page page and a 50k FCFA regional starter are different bundles. Verify effort, recurring costs, local purchasing power, tax/payment friction and support before setting any price floor.

## Opica pricing position and next calculation

**Position: Unknown / not benchmarkable yet.** The public portfolio includes project summaries but no charges, dates, scope documents or production/service status. There is no auditable basis to state where Opica is priced competitively or expensively.

Before setting list prices, for each proposed package calculate:

`minimum quote = expected delivery hours × owner-approved loaded hourly cost + direct third-party costs + risk/contingency + desired contribution margin`

Then compare a like-for-like scope checklist: pages/screens, languages, CMS/admin, payment method, content writing, hosting/domain, deployment, source ownership, training, revisions, warranty, support, tax, and client inputs. Consider a quote-only approach for custom products, with optional published “from” prices only after a margin and scope audit.
